Parser: month-first interpretation for day-first tenants

The shared parser treats numeric dates as month first. A tenant whose locale is day first can submit 07/04/2026 for 7 April and receive 4 July. Tenant settings, rather than the reporter’s browser locale, determine how the date should be read.

Reproduce with a day-first tenant on the app or email path, or the booking-v2 form. Capture the entered date, stored date and generated invite before changing a booking. The expected result preserves the date intended under the tenant’s locale.

The parser issue covers every report the supplied classifier calls parser, including the explicitly flagged one-day descriptions. Keep that attribution qualified until their original inputs and timestamps are checked.

Hint: booking-v3 instructs the opposite date order

For day-first tenants, booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY, while its form reads dates in the tenant’s day-first order. For example, a user follows that hint and enters 04/07/2026 for 7 April; a day-first form reads 4 July. Before retirement, the field instruction and its examples needed to agree with the form parser. Changing the shared parser without checking this form would not prove that the hint problem was fixed. The desk instead reports the form retired and the hint outcome as won’t fix.

Reproduce on the form channel for Alder Physio (de-DE), Copper Studio (es-ES) or Pale Harbour (pt-PT). Check the hint shown, original input and parsed value. App and email reports from those tenants still use the parser classification. A tenant name alone is insufficient to group a report.

What the timeline measures
